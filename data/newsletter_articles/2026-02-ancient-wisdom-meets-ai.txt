## Future of Learning | When 3,000-Year-Old Grammar Meets Modern AI
p: We typically imagine Large Language Models (LLMs) as products of the chaotic modern internet. But a ground breaking initiative in India is flipping the script by training AI on one of the world's most structured languages: Sanskrit.

## The Mylapore Initiative
p: Based at the historic MDS Sanskrit College in Chennai, researchers are collaborating with IIT Madras to treat Sanskrit as a **computational framework** rather than just text to be translated.
p: By feeding over 110,000 rare manuscripts into the model, they are encoding linguistic rules—like *sandhi* (phonetic blending)—directly into the AI. This turns Chennai into a vibrant hub where digital heritage meets cutting-edge tech.

## Open Source Breakthroughs
p: The community isn't waiting around. The release of **Sanskrit-Qwen2.5-7B** on Hugging Face is a game-changer. This 7-billion parameter model can generate coherent Sanskrit passages, proving that we don't always need massive models to master complex, rule-based languages.

## Practical Takeaway
hl: **Structure is key.** The Sanskrit LLM succeeds because it respects the inherent grammatical rules of the data. When applying AI to your own educational content, clean and structure your source material first. Quality rules often beat quantity of data.

## 🚨 AI Glitch
p: **The Scenario:** An Air Canada passenger asked the chatbot about bereavement fares. The bot invented a policy allowing retroactive refunds, contradicting the airline's official rules.
p: **The Company's Defence:** They argued they weren't liable for the chatbot's hallucinations.
p: **The Verdict:** The tribunal ruled that Air Canada is responsible for all information on its site, including AI responses.
